CEPHALOPODS

The intelligent shape-shifters of the deep…

🦑 GLASS SQUID

Cranchiidae · ~60 species

Drifting silently through the twilight zone, the glass squid is nature's living crystal. Almost invisible in the deep, it carries every secret of its strange biology on full display.

  • Transparent glowing body — almost completely see-through, with bioluminescent organs lighting up its silhouette
  • Food travels right through their brain — the esophagus literally passes through the donut-shaped brain on its way to the stomach
  • THREE hearts — one main systemic heart plus two branchial hearts pumping blood through the gills
  • Can turn completely black in a flash — special pigment cells flood the body to vanish into the darkness when threatened

🐙 OCTOPUS

Octopoda · over 300 species

Eight arms, three hearts, blue blood, and a brain in every limb — the octopus is so alien it has been called the closest thing to intelligent life from another planet.

  • 8 arms with hundreds of suckers — each sucker can taste, grip, and even smell what it touches
  • 3 hearts and blue blood — one main heart pumps to the body, two more push blood through the gills; a copper-based blood pigment makes it blue
  • Super intelligent — solves puzzles, opens jars, escapes tanks, and even uses tools like coconut shells for armour
  • Can change color instantly — special skin cells called chromatophores fire in milliseconds to camouflage, communicate, or warn

🦑 GIANT SQUID

Architeuthis dux

For centuries it was a sailor's tale — a colossal beast lurking in the deep, dragging ships beneath the waves. The Giant Squid is real, and almost as strange as the legend.

  • Biggest eyes of any animal on Earth — up to 27 cm across, each the size of a dinner plate, perfect for spotting tiny flickers of light in the abyss
  • Lives in the deep ocean — patrols the cold midnight zone hundreds to thousands of metres below the surface
  • Rarely seen alive — was never filmed in its natural habitat until 2012; almost everything we know comes from washed-up bodies
  • Can stretch up to 13 metres long from mantle tip to feeding-tentacle tip

🦑 COLOSSAL SQUID

Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni

Heavier than the Giant Squid, armed with rotating hooks instead of plain suckers, the Colossal Squid haunts the freezing waters around Antarctica.

  • Largest invertebrate alive — weighs up to 750 kg, more massive than any creature without a backbone on the planet
  • Has rotating hooks, not suckers — its two long feeding tentacles bristle with swivelling claws perfect for gripping struggling prey
  • Lives deep in the freezing Southern Ocean around Antarctica, mostly below 1,000 m
  • Locked in an ancient battle with sperm whales — circular sucker scars cover their attackers' skin

🐚 NAUTILUS

Nautilidae · 6 living species

Hidden inside a perfect spiral shell, the nautilus has drifted through the oceans almost unchanged for nearly half a billion years — a creature older than the dinosaurs.

  • Living fossil unchanged for 500 million years — its body plan has barely shifted since long before the first land plants existed
  • Its shell is a perfect logarithmic spiral — divided into chambers it adjusts with gas to float, sink, or hover
  • Has up to 90 sticky tentacles — but no suckers, unlike its squid and octopus cousins
  • The only living cephalopod with an external shell

🦑 CUTTLEFISH

Sepiidae

The artists of the cephalopod world. With skin that paints itself in shimmering patterns and pupils shaped like the letter W, cuttlefish hypnotise prey and predators alike.

  • Masters of camouflage — fire millions of pigment cells called chromatophores in milliseconds to vanish into any background, even matching textures and patterns
  • Famous W-shaped pupils for amazing depth perception
  • Probably colour-blind themselves — yet still match colour perfectly using polarised light
  • Some hypnotise prey with shifting black-and-white waves rolling across their skin

🐙 THE KRAKEN 🐙

Largest. Oldest. Most terrifying of all…

Some say it was just a sailor's nightmare. Others say it still waits in the lightless trench, eight massive arms ready to drag the world below the waves. The legendary Kraken has terrified mariners for a thousand years — and the truth may be stranger than the myth.
